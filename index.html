What changed in this edition

  • Foothills Fire & Rescue updated its CWPP in December 2020. Our last edition relied on its 2008 plan. Lookout Mountain, Mount Vernon Canyon and Idledale now reflect the 2020 hazard zones, and Idledale moved up from High to Very High.
  • Published ISO classes were corrected for Genesee, Foothills Fire & Rescue, Evergreen and West Metro.
  • Jefferson County's new Wildfire Resiliency Code took effect July 1, 2026. It affects what a roof, deck or siding replacement will cost.
  • Modeled evacuation times were removed. We could not tie them to current source documents, and we would rather show you nothing than a number we can't stand behind.
  • Where we could not confirm a figure, the guide now says "Not confirmed" instead of carrying an old number forward.

How to use this guide

This is due-diligence support, not a wildfire risk assessment, engineering evaluation or insurance advice, and it does not guarantee safety, evacuation success or insurability. Conditions, codes and ratings change. Before buying in the wildland-urban interface (WUI), confirm details with the fire district that serves the address, get quotes from more than one insurance carrier, and consider a site visit from a wildfire mitigation specialist.

Two things that changed the math in 2026

Building code

Jefferson County Wildfire Resiliency Code

The county commissioners approved the code on March 10, 2026, along with a new WUI Overlay District map. It took effect July 1, 2026.16 It applies only to unincorporated Jefferson County inside the WUI overlay, and the new map expands the WUI boundary.1617

It is not a retrofit mandate. It applies to new construction and to exterior work on existing homes such as reroofing, re-siding, deck replacement and additions.17

What it means for a buyer: if the roof, deck or siding is near the end of its life, assume the replacement will need to meet the new standard and price that into your offer. Homes inside town limits, like the Town of Morrison, fall under that town's code, and some Evergreen addresses are in Clear Creek County, so confirm which jurisdiction applies.

Insurance

The Colorado FAIR Plan is a backstop, not a plan

Colorado's insurer of last resort began taking residential applications on April 10, 2025. Residential coverage is capped at $750,000 and pays actual cash value rather than full replacement cost.20 It requires proof that private carriers have declined you, and it excludes liability coverage, so a separate policy is needed for that.21

What it means for a buyer: get a quote from a standard carrier before your inspection deadline, not after. On a foothills home that would cost well over $750,000 to rebuild, a FAIR Plan policy can leave a real gap, and your lender will want to see coverage before closing.

The four terms you'll see

ISO class

A 1 to 10 grade of a community's fire protection, where 1 is best and 10 means no recognized protection. Many districts publish a split class like 3/10. The first number generally applies within 5 road miles of a station and 1,000 feet of a hydrant, the second within 5 road miles but farther than 1,000 feet from a hydrant, and Class 10 generally applies beyond 5 road miles.4 Your insurer, not the fire district, sets the premium. Ask your agent which class they have on file for the address.

CWPP

A Community Wildfire Protection Plan is a public document most fire districts publish and update every several years. It breaks the district into hazard zones or plan units, rates each one, and recommends specific work on fuels, roads, water and home hardening. It is the most candid public document about a neighborhood's wildfire risk, and it's free.

Hazard zones and plan units

Most current CWPPs rate small areas, not whole communities. One neighborhood name can include a Low zone and a Very High zone half a mile apart. That is why this guide shows ranges and why the zone for the specific address matters more than the neighborhood label.

Firewise USA

An NFPA program that recognizes neighborhoods that complete a risk assessment, adopt an action plan and log mitigation work every year. Some insurers recognize it. We didn't confirm current Firewise status for every neighborhood here, so check the NFPA's site or ask the fire district.

Genesee

Our read: High

Strong local fire protection for a mountain community, in a dense forest setting where the 2021 plan found no safe place to shelter if leaving isn't possible.

Genesee Fire Rescue reports an ISO class of 3, which it describes as top 15% nationally.1 The district updated its CWPP in 2021 with new fire and evacuation modeling.2 The community's own wildfire page reports that the 2021 plan looked for areas where residents could safely shelter in place and found none.3

That last point is the one buyers should sit with. In Genesee, the plan is to leave early. Your evacuation readiness matters as much as the home's construction.

Our take: one of the better-prepared communities in this guide, still in a high-risk setting. Read the 2021 CWPP for the plan unit that contains the address, and sign up for LookoutAlert before closing.

Lookout Mountain

Our read: High, ranging Low to Very High

What the 2020 plan says

Paradise Hills is rated Low, with multiple ways in and out and hydrants. The Panorama area and the homes off Indian Paintbrush Drive are rated Moderate. Homes off Krestview Lane and Hess Avenue, and the area reached by Colorow Road, are rated High. Cody Park is rated Very High: mostly dirt roads that are steep and narrow, heavy forest up to the road edge, few turnarounds, and a single hydrant on a small supply line.5

Across the district, the plan found most homes lack defensible space and recommends that every home have it.5

Our take: on Lookout Mountain the street matters more than the neighborhood name. Find the hazard zone for the address on the district's interactive CWPP map before you write an offer.

Mount Vernon Canyon

Our read: High, ranging Moderate to Extreme

Most of Mount Vernon Country Club rates Moderate with hydrants and more than one exit, but its north end, the US-40 corridor and the Rainbow Hills area rate much higher.

Hydrants in Mount Vernon Country Club; cisterns or ponds in several other zones5

What the 2020 plan says

Most homes in Mount Vernon Country Club sit in a Moderate zone with good roads, multiple exits and hydrants. The north end of the neighborhood is rated Very High: older homes and cabins, mostly dirt roads, and every road out converging at a single point. The neighborhood between US-40 and I-70 is rated High, and the plan lists two small ponds as its only water source.5

At the northwest end of the district, Rainbow Hills and the Moss Rock Road and Summit Ranch area are rated Extreme. Both are single-access, heavily forested, and depend on cisterns rather than hydrants.5 The Mount Vernon Metro District has been building a fuel break along the north and east sides of the country club.5

Our take: a wide spread within a short drive. A home in the main part of the country club and a home in Rainbow Hills are different risk decisions.

Evergreen Fire/Rescue lists eight station locations. Station 4 is EMS-only and not used for ISO purposes, and the district notes that hydrants in the Brook Forest area and at Marshdale Elementary are not recognized by ISO.6 That detail can change the class your insurer applies.

Evergreen spans two counties, so an Evergreen address may fall under Clear Creek County rules rather than Jefferson County's.

Our take: treat "Evergreen" as a dozen different neighborhoods. Find the plan unit, find its Wildfire Ambassador, and ask what work has actually been done on that street.

West Metro is a large, full-time department with more than 400 firefighters across 17 stations.10 That is a real asset in the town core. The foothills homes around Morrison are a different picture, with steeper terrain, heavier fuel and longer driveways. Morrison is an incorporated town, so Jefferson County's new code doesn't apply inside town limits; ask the town about its own requirements.

Our take: evaluate the town core and the surrounding hillsides as separate decisions. Confirm which fire district serves the address on the county assessor's record.

The department reports average response times under three minutes.13 The wildfire exposure here is mostly fast-moving grass fire from open space to the west and from South Table Mountain, not a forest fire front. Moderate is not the same as no risk. Grass fires move quickly and wind-driven embers don't respect neighborhood lines.

Our take: the best fit in this guide for buyers who want foothills access with the most evacuation confidence. Still do the five-foot zone around the house.

Small hydrant systems in the Aspen Park and Conifer commercial areas19; tankers and cisterns elsewhere

Elk Creek reports that since a 2016 automatic aid agreement, properties within 5 miles of any participating station, including neighboring districts' stations, rate Class 5, and it holds ISO tanker credit for areas beyond hydrant reach.15 That helps insurability in an area where most homes aren't near a hydrant.

The 2012 Lower North Fork Fire, an escaped prescribed burn, burned roughly 4,100 acres south of Conifer, killed three residents and destroyed more than 20 homes.2425 Elk Creek and Inter-Canyon now run a shared wildland division and a free community chipping program.18

Our take: serious due diligence territory. Find the plan unit in the 2021 CWPP, confirm the distance to the nearest station for ISO purposes, and get quotes before you're under contract.

The Quarry Fire started the night of July 30, 2024 along Deer Creek Canyon Road and was fully contained August 7 at 579.6 acres. No homes were lost, and the sheriff's office investigated it as possible arson.27 About 575 homes were under mandatory evacuation, including the Deer Creek Mesa, Sampson and Maxwell subdivisions.2829

How we rated these neighborhoods

No formula. Our read is a comparative judgment built from the current CWPP for each district, district publications, and recent fire history. We weighted three things most: how many real ways out a neighborhood has, how close heavy fuel sits to homes, and how much water firefighters can reach.

Where a CWPP rates multiple zones inside one neighborhood, the range in the chart shows the spread and the dot shows our overall read. Where we couldn't confirm a source for this edition, we say so instead of filling the gap. When the evidence was uncertain, we leaned conservative.

These ratings are not a probability of fire, a prediction about any specific home, or insurance underwriting guidance.
